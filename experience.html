Trackside Engineering Placement - Red Bull Powertrains

July 2025 - July 2026

During my 12-month placement at Red Bull Powertrains, I worked within the Trackside Engineering team supporting the development, validation, and operation of Red Bull's first power unit. My work was split between Product Validation and Confirmation & Durability, giving me experience across both data-driven engineering and power unit testing.

Product Validation & Data Science

I worked extensively with Formula 1 power unit telemetry, developing models and analysis tools to support both race operations and the development of the new 2026 power unit. This included developing a lumped-parameter thermal model of the MGU-K that was deployed on the race ECU, achieving temperature predictions within ±1 °C during live running on track.

I performed competitor analysis by reverse-engineering energy deployment profiles of qualifying laps from onboard audio using FFT and spectrogram analysis. This information was used to give comparison against our own deployment profiles and feed into our lap simulation. In addition, I developed a genetic algorithm optimizer to determine laptime-optimal power unit allocation across a race season for a given car, dynamically updating the allocation as races progressed.

Alongside modelling and analysis, I created specified test run plans for over 200 laps of transient dyno running, optimized internal telemetry libraries for Python, and developed a Streamlit-based telemetry visualization platform deployed on a Kubernetes cluster, allowing engineers across the factory to interactively generate custom plots from power unit data.

Confirmation & Durability

In the Confirmation & Durability team, I operated Formula 1 power units on dynamometer test cells for durability testing and new engine pass-off programs. I monitored real-time telemetry across the ICE, turbo, MGU-K, and battery systems using ATLAS and System Monitor, investigating faults and anomalies during testing.

When issues occurred, I worked across different power unit subsystems to identify root causes, coordinated with designers to determine solutions, and developed diagnostic methodology for recurring faults.

I also owned the power unit operational limits document, defining safe operating boundaries used by factory and trackside teams. Values were updated as spec changes, calibration releases, and discoveries from dyno running were made, communicating new releases to the race team on a race by race basis.

During dyno downtime, I developed a dyno hours monitoring dashboard, tracking Operational and Occupancy hours, mileage, fuel consumption for each dyno, alongside totals for V6 and single cylinder dyno. These metrics were updated live, and were also available for each engine serial. This was parallel to my work in creating dyno hour reports across each period, used for legality checks by the FIA.
